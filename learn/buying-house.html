Buying a House: Installments vs. Lump Sum

By Financogram  ·  4 min read

If you're lucky enough to have saved up a significant amount of capital, you might be facing the ultimate real estate dilemma: should you buy a house outright in cash, or take a mortgage (installments) and invest your extra cash elsewhere?

It sounds counterintuitive. Why take on a 20-year loan and pay millions in interest if you already have the money in your bank account? The answer comes down to one of the most powerful concepts in finance: Opportunity Cost.

The Case for Buying in Cash (Lump Sum)

Buying a house completely in cash gives you the ultimate peace of mind. Your home is 100% yours, nobody can foreclose on it, and your monthly expenses drop drastically because you have no rent or EMI to pay.

The Pros:

  • No Interest: You save an enormous amount of money that would otherwise go to the bank as interest.
  • Lower Stress: Job loss is much less terrifying when you don't have a massive monthly loan payment hanging over your head.
  • Better Negotiation: Cash buyers can often negotiate better purchase prices.

The Cons:

  • All your wealth is tied up in a single, highly illiquid asset.
  • You lose out on the potential stock market returns you could have made with that money.

The Case for Installments (Mortgage + Investing)

When you take a mortgage, you are using the bank's money to buy the house, keeping your own cash free. You then take your cash and invest it in the stock market (or mutual funds).

The math works like this: If the bank charges you 10% interest on your loan, but you can earn 15% interest by investing your cash in the PSX, you are profiting 5% on the difference. This is called leverage.

The Pros:

  • Higher Net Worth: Historically, stock markets yield higher returns than mortgage interest rates over a 20-year period.
  • Liquidity: Your cash is sitting in stocks/funds, which you can sell in 2 days if you need emergency cash. You can't sell a "bedroom" if you suddenly need money.

The Cons:

  • You take on debt, which carries a psychological burden.
  • If the market crashes or your investments underperform your interest rate, you lose money.

The Verdict

Mathematically, taking an installment plan (at a reasonable interest rate) and investing your cash usually results in a higher net worth after 20 years. However, personal finance is personal. If carrying a mortgage makes you lose sleep at night, buy it in cash. Peace of mind yields the best return on investment.
