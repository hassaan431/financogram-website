Gold vs Stocks: What 20 Years of Real Pakistani Data Actually Shows

Every family in Pakistan has an opinion on this one. Your uncle swears by gold. Your cousin who "plays the market" swears by stocks. Who's actually right?

We pulled 20 years of real numbers — gold prices and the KSE-100 — to find out.

The 20-year scoreboard

Put Rs 100,000 into gold in 2005, and by 2025 it's worth roughly Rs 18.7 lakh.

Put that same Rs 100,000 into the KSE-100 in 2005, and it's worth roughly Rs 18.2 lakh.

That's basically a tie — about 15–16% a year, either way. If that's all you looked at, you'd conclude it doesn't matter which one you pick.

You'd be wrong.

Zoom into any 10-year window and the story flips

The 20-year average hides two very different decades:

  • 2010–2020: stocks grew about 13.8% a year. Gold managed just 6.4% a year — it barely moved for long stretches.
  • 2020–2025: gold roared back with 29.5% a year, edging out stocks' still-strong 31.8%.

So "gold vs stocks" isn't really a 20-year question. It's a "which decade are you living through" question — and nobody knows that in advance.

The one number both of them beat

Here's the actually reassuring part: inflation over the same 20 years needed your money to grow about 10.3% a year just to keep its value. Both gold and stocks cleared that bar by a wide margin. The real risk in Pakistan has never been "gold vs stocks" — it's sitting in cash and letting inflation eat it.

What this means for you

  • Neither asset is a guaranteed winner over any given decade — that's exactly why financial advisors keep saying "diversify," even though it sounds like a cliché.
  • If you need the money in the next 5 years, recent history matters more than the 20-year average.
  • If you're investing for the long run, both have historically outrun inflation by a comfortable margin — the bigger mistake is not investing at all.
